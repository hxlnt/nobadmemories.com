|----------|----------------|
| _LAPIS_ 1966<BR>10 minutes; 16mm movie film<BR>By: James Whitney at the filmmaker’s L. A. Studio<BR>Equipment: Mechanical analog machine (prototype) | |
| _TERMINAL SELF_ 1971<BR>8 minutes; 16 mm movie film<BR>By: John Whitney, Jr. at the filmmaker’s L. A. Studio<BR>Equipment: Mechanical analog machine (prototype) | |
| _YIN HSIEN_ 1976<BR>9 minutes; 16 mm movie film<BR>By: Michael Whitney at the filmmaker’s L. A. Studio<BR>Equipment: Motion-controlled optical printer (prototype) | |
| _BINARY BIT PATTERNS_ 1968<BR>3 minutes; 16 mm movie film<BR>By: Michael Whitney and John Whitney, Sr. at Information International<BR>Equipment: FR80 Microfilm Plotter | |
| _PERMUTATIONS_ 1968<BR>7 minutes; 16 mm movie film<BR>By: John Whitney, Sr. at UCLA Health Sciences<BR>Equipment: IBM 2250 | |
| _FIRST FIG_ 1974<BR>6 minutes; 16 mm movie film<BR>By: Larry Cube and Gary Imhoff at Cal Arts<BR>Equipment: Univac 1108, SC 4020 Microfilm Plotter | |
| _GENESYS_ c. 1969<BR>25 minutes (short excerpt); 16 mm movie film<BR>By: Ronald Baecker, Eric Martin and Lynn Smith at MIT Lincoln Labs<BR>Equipment: TX-2<BR>Illustrates the use of the Genesys computer animation system. | |
| _COMPUTER COLOR GENERATIONS_ 1972<BR>23 minutes; 16 mm movie film with sound<BR>By: Los Alamos Scientific Laboratory<BR>Equipment: FR80 Microfilm Recorder<BR>A documentary about a technique developed at Los Alamos by which computers can produce color film output at the same cost as black-and-white output. Using computer movie runs as examples, the advantages of color over black and white are discussed. | |
| _RON HAYS MUSIC-IMAGE PRESENTATION_ 1974-1975<BR>50 min; 3/4-inch videotape; sound<BR>By: Ron Hays at WGBH, Boston; Hollywood, CA; and New York City<BR>Equipment: CMX computer editor; PAIK-ABE video synthesizer; Scanimate computer; various video switchers and special effects generators plus computer-controlled animation techniques.<BR>Presentation includes overview of Ron Hays’ work in visual music and multi-media concert situations. Selections include works as seen on public television, feature-length films (_Sergeant Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band_ and _Demon Seed_) plus television coverage of _Star Wars_ concerts at Montreal Olympic Stadium, 62,000 people in attendance, using lasers and computer imagery. Presentation also includes examples of Emmy award-winning computer graphic title sequences. | |
| _MEASURE FOR MEASURE_ 1968<BR>14 minutes; 3/4-inch videotape<BR>By: Animation and Computer Graphics Staff at the New York Institute of Technology<BR>Equipment: PDP 11s, Evans and Sutherland frame buffers, Three River Graphic Display Processors.<BR>An animated film that pokes fun at our way of measuring. Two kinds of computer animation and conventional animation were intercut. | |
| _NYIT SAMPLERS_ 1976<BR>10 minutes; 35 mm movie film and 3/4-inch videotape<BR>By: The New York Institute of Technology Computer Graphics Laboratory<BR>A collection of animated pieces made at NYIT including 2-d and 3-d animation, special effects, and commercials. | |
| _HALLEY FLYBY / TEMPEL 2 RENDEZVOUS_ 1978<BR>12 minutes; 16 mm movie film; silent<BR>By: Linda M. Lee, James F. Blinn, and Julian E. Gomez at the Jet Propulsion Laboratory<BR>Equipment: Evans and Sutherland Picture System 2, PDP 11/55, Univac 1108, Calcomp Microfilm<BR>A study of a proposed mission to encounter Halley’s Comet and Comet Tempel 2. | *The proposed Halley/Tempel 2 mission did not occur, and the spacecraft instead became* Mariner 5 *as part of a NASA mission to study the atmosphere of Venus.* |
| _ANIMA II_ 1977<BR>20 minutes; 3/4-inch videotape<BR>By: Computer Graphics Research Group at Ohio State University<BR>Equipment: PDP-11/45, Video display device<BR>3-d color raster graphics animation system.<BR> Demonstrates 3-d data generation and user specification and control of animation with real-time playback capability to a standard color television set. | |
| _VISUAL LEARNING_ 1979<BR>8 minutes; 3/4-inch videotape<BR>By: Tom Linehan and Charles Csuri at Ohio State University<BR>Equipment: PDP-11/45, Video display device<BR>Application of art education. | |
| _PIXILLATION_ 1970<BR>4 minutes; 16 mm movie film; sound<BR>By: Ken Knowlton and Lillian Schwartz at Bell Laboratories, Murray Hill, NJ<BR>Equipment: SC 4020 Microfilm Plotter, optically added color<BR>Abstract art film. | |
| _ABSTRACTIONS ON A BEDSHEET_ 1972<BR>7 minutes; 3/4-inch videotape; sound<BR>By: Bill Etra in Miami, Florida and New York City<BR>Equipment: PDP 11, oscillators<BR>An abstract art work with computer-controlled audio and computer-controlled programmed distortion of a video raster. | |
| _OLYMPIAD_ 1971<BR>3-1/2 minutes; 16 mm movie film; sound<BR>By: Ken Knowlton and Lillian Schwartz at Bell Laboratories, Murray Hill, NJ<BR>Equipment: SC 4020 Microfilm Plotter, optically added color; BEFLIX and EXPLOR software systems.<BR>Abstract art film based on programmed manipulation of running man image. | |
| _MS MUFFET_ 1975<BR>3 minutes; 3/4-inch videotape; sound<BR>By: Lou Katz, Bill Etra, and Louise Etra at Columbia University and Etras’ studio<BR>Equipment: PDP 11/45; Tektronix Scan Converter at 4010 terminal; Rutt-Etra Video Synthesizer; Electronic Associates of Berkeley Video Lab; Synthi Audio Synthesizer.<BR>A real-time interactive art piece, with computer-controlled graphics, colorization, sound and keying effects. The performer interacted with the images as the piece was recorded. | |
| _PATCHWORK ’71_ Prior to 1971<BR>30 minutes; 16 mm movie film; soundCollected and Edited <BR>By: Professor Kent Wilson, Department of Chemistry, University of California, San Diego<BR>Equipment: Many systems. Some sound and optical color added by Dr. Wilson.<BR>A sampler of excerpts from instructional and demo films produced by many of the graphics groups active in the early ’70’s in several universities and laboratories. | |
| _FLEXIPEDE_ 1972<BR>1 minute; 16 mm movie film<BR>By: Tony Pritchett at ATLAS Lab<BR>Equipment: SD 4020<BR>Fun. An early example of computer animation. | |
| _THE PRISM SHOWREEL_ 1976-1979<BR>4 minutes; 3/4-inch videotape<BR>By: Peter Chandler, Tony Diment, and Hayden Young at Imperial College, London; EMI Central Research Labs, Hayes; and EMI A/V.<BR>Equipment: PDP 11/40<BR>Selected title sequences and commercials. | |
